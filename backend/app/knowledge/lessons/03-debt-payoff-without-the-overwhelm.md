## Two common ways to choose a focus

When you have several debts, it can be hard to decide where extra repayment money should go. Two approaches often discussed are the snowball and the avalanche. They are ways of organising repayment, not a substitute for covering essential costs or understanding which debts have serious consequences if left unpaid.

With the snowball approach, you focus extra money on the debt with the smallest balance while keeping up with agreed payments on other debts where possible. Clearing a smaller balance can create a visible milestone and may make it easier to stay engaged. The trade-off is that the smaller debt may not be the one costing you the most in interest.

With the avalanche approach, you focus extra money on the debt with the highest interest cost, while maintaining other required payments. This can reduce the amount of interest paid compared with directing the same extra amount to a lower-cost debt, assuming the rates and terms stay as expected. The highest-cost debt may also have a large balance, so progress can feel slower at first.

## Choose a method you can keep using

Neither method is automatically right for everyone. A plan that is mathematically efficient but too stressful to follow may not suit you. On the other hand, motivation matters, and a quick early win can help some people continue.

Before choosing, make a list of each debt, its balance, interest or charges, payment due and any important terms. Check the information with the lender or provider rather than relying on memory. If rates or fees can change, note that too. A repayment calculator can illustrate possible differences, but its result depends on the figures entered and is not a guarantee.

Keep up with payments required by your agreements if you can. If that is not possible, contact the organisations you owe as soon as you can and ask about options. Do not assume a repayment strategy can safely ignore a missed payment or a debt with serious consequences.

## Make progress visible without blame

Choose a review rhythm that feels manageable, such as checking balances when statements arrive. Record what has changed and what your next step is. If a payment goes mainly towards interest or charges, that does not mean you have made no effort; it may mean the plan needs another look.

If your circumstances are tight or you are unsure which debts to deal with first, seek free, impartial guidance before committing to a new plan. MoneyHelper and StepChange provide UK information and routes to support. A good strategy should fit your whole situation, including housing, essential bills and wellbeing, rather than focusing on a single balance in isolation.
