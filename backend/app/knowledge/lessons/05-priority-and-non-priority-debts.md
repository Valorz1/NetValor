## Priority is about consequences

People often use the terms priority and non-priority to describe debts, but the labels are about the consequences of not paying, not about whether a debt matters. A debt that is called non-priority is still a real obligation. The distinction can help people decide what needs attention first when there is not enough money to pay everything.

Some unpaid bills can put essential services, housing or important belongings at risk, or lead to formal action with serious effects. Examples can include housing costs, certain utility bills, council tax and some court-related payments. The exact consequences depend on the type of debt, the rules that apply and individual circumstances. Do not rely on a general list as a final decision about your own case.

Other debts, such as many credit agreements, can still lead to charges, contact from creditors, credit-file effects or legal action if ignored. Calling them non-priority does not mean they can be forgotten or that you should stop paying without understanding the agreement and getting advice.

## Make an informed list

Write down who is owed, the amount, what the payment is for, when it is due and what communication you have received. Keep letters and notices together, including anything that mentions a deadline or court process. If you are not sure what a document means, ask for help promptly rather than waiting.

Think first about immediate needs and serious consequences. If a payment is not affordable, contact the organisation involved and explain your situation. Ask what options are available and keep a record of the conversation. An affordable arrangement is generally more realistic than agreeing to an amount you cannot maintain.

## Get help with the order

The right order can depend on where in the UK you live, what the debt is, and whether formal action has started. Rules and processes can differ across the UK, so check current guidance for your location. MoneyHelper provides information about prioritising debts, and StepChange offers free debt advice. Citizens Advice can also help you understand notices and local options.

Do not ignore a court document, eviction warning, disconnection notice or other urgent communication. Seek specialist advice quickly, especially if you are worried about losing your home or an essential service. You do not need to solve every debt at once; the first useful step may be identifying the most urgent issue and getting support to deal with it.

Once immediate risks are understood, you can consider how to manage other payments and whether a longer-term repayment arrangement is suitable. A clear list and informed support can make an overwhelming situation feel more manageable without pretending that every debt has identical consequences.
